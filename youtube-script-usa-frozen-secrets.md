**Format:** Faceless documentary-style facts video (stock footage, maps, 3D renders, archival photos, on-screen text)
**Length:** ~10–11 min (~1,500 words at 145 wpm)
**Voice:** Calm, curious, slightly ominous US-English narrator. Short sentences. No filler.
**Target audience:** US viewers 18–44, "fascinating facts / mystery / science" lane

Stay to the end, because number one... *could change how you think about the planet.*

### [0:50–1:50] #10 — ÖTZI THE ICEMAN
**VISUAL:** Alps footage, museum reconstruction, 3D render of the axe and arrow wounds.

**NARRATOR:**
Number ten. Ötzi, the Iceman.
Found in the Ötztal Alps, he lived around 3300 BC. His skin, his clothes, even his last meal were preserved.
Scientists found an arrowhead in his shoulder. He wasn't a lost hiker. He was likely **murdered**, making this the world's oldest cold case.
**RE-HOOK:** But a frozen murder is nothing compared to what's coming.

### [1:50–2:50] #9 — A WWII BOMBER, "GLACIER GIRL"
**VISUAL:** Greenland ice, archival photos of P-38 Lightnings.

**NARRATOR:**
In 1942, a squad of US warplanes crash-landed on Greenland's ice. Everyone survived.
Fifty years later, a team went back. The planes were buried under **268 feet** of ice.
They dug one out, piece by piece, and flew it again.
The ice had moved the aircraft miles from where it landed. Lesson: ice is not still. It travels.

### [2:50–3:50] #8 — ANCIENT VIRUSES
**VISUAL:** Microscope footage, Siberian permafrost.

**NARRATOR:**
In 2014, French scientists revived a giant virus from Siberian permafrost. It had been frozen for roughly **30,000 years**.
It only infects amoebas, so we're safe. For now.
Researchers have since reported even older viral material. As the permafrost thaws, nobody fully knows what else is waiting.
**RE-HOOK:** Number seven is worse. It's not a virus. It's something we built.

### [3:50–5:00] #7 — CAMP CENTURY: THE US ARMY'S SECRET CITY
**VISUAL:** Declassified photos, tunnels, 3D cutaway of the base.

**NARRATOR:**
In 1959, the US Army built a secret base **under the Greenland ice**, with tunnels, a hospital, and a nuclear reactor.
Officially it was a science station. Secretly it was "Project Iceworm", a plan to hide nuclear missiles close to the Soviet Union.
The ice moved and crushed the tunnels, and the Army abandoned the base in 1967.
They left behind waste, and the assumption that snow would bury it forever.
Climate scientists now warn that melting could expose it by the end of the century. *(Say "studies suggest", not "will".)*

### [5:00–6:00] #6 — MAMMOTHS WITH LIQUID BLOOD
**VISUAL:** Mammoth "Yuka" photos, lab shots.

**NARRATOR:**
Number six. In 2013, Russian researchers found a mammoth so well preserved that **dark liquid blood flowed** when they cut into it.
Some teams have since explored whether such cells could help bring mammoth traits back to life.
Not Jurassic Park. But closer than you think.

### [6:00–7:00] #5 — THE LOST BABY MAMMOTH, LYUBA
**VISUAL:** Lyuba, museum display.

**NARRATOR:**
Lyuba, a baby mammoth, was found in 2007 and is about **42,000 years old**.
She had mud in her trunk, which suggests she suffocated.
Her last meal? Her mother's milk and feces, which baby elephants eat for gut bacteria. Even in death, she told us how mammoths raised their young.

### [7:00–8:00] #4 — ANTARCTIC LAKE VOSTOK
**VISUAL:** Antarctica map, drilling rig, 3D lake cross-section.

**NARRATOR:**
Four miles below the Antarctic surface lies **Lake Vostok**, sealed off for millions of years.
Russian drillers reached it in 2012. Samples contained signs of bacterial DNA.
A lake with no sunlight and no contact with our world. If life survives there, it could survive on Europa too. NASA is paying attention.

### [8:00–9:00] #3 — THE FROZEN FOREST
**VISUAL:** Axel Heiberg Island, ancient stumps.

**NARRATOR:**
On Canada's Axel Heiberg Island, you can walk through a forest that's **45 million years old**.
Trees were buried standing, then preserved. The Arctic was once warm enough for swamp cypress. This is Earth's own time capsule of climate change.

### [9:00–9:45] #2 — THE LOST FRANKLIN EXPEDITION
**VISUAL:** Ships HMS Erebus/Terror, sonar, historic illustrations.

**NARRATOR:**
In 1845, two British ships vanished into the Arctic with 129 men.
Searchers spent 170 years looking. The ships were finally found in 2014 and 2016, nearly intact on the seafloor, so well preserved that cabin items were still in place.
Why did they never come home? Lead poisoning, starvation, and ice that wouldn't let go.

### [9:45–10:30] #1 — THE OLDEST ICE ON EARTH
**VISUAL:** Ice cores, lab, bubbles under microscope.

**NARRATOR:**
And number one: ice itself.
Scientists in Antarctica have drilled ice cores that are over **a million years old**, and some projects aim for more than 1.5 million.
Each tiny bubble is a sealed sample of ancient air. It shows us exactly how carbon dioxide and temperature moved together across history.
This ice is a library. And it's melting.
